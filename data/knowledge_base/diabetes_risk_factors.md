# Diabetes Risk Factors and Pathophysiology

## What is Type 2 Diabetes?
Type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) is a chronic metabolic disorder characterized
by hyperglycemia (high blood glucose) resulting from insulin resistance and
progressive insulin secretion deficiency. It accounts for 90–95% of all
diabetes cases worldwide.

---

## Core Mechanism: Insulin Resistance

### What is Insulin Resistance?
Insulin resistance is a condition where cells in the muscles, fat, and liver
do not respond well to insulin and cannot efficiently take up glucose from
the blood. As a result:

1. Pancreatic beta cells produce MORE insulin to compensate
2. Blood glucose remains elevated despite high insulin levels
3. Eventually beta cells become exhausted
4. Insulin production drops → overt diabetes develops

### Causes of Insulin Resistance:
- **Obesity:** Excess adipose tissue, especially visceral fat, releases
  free fatty acids and inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-6) that directly
  interfere with insulin signaling pathways
- **Physical inactivity:** Muscle is a primary site of glucose uptake;
  sedentary lifestyle reduces muscle insulin sensitivity
- **Diet:** High-calorie, refined carbohydrate diets cause chronic
  hyperglycemia and hyperinsulinemia, accelerating resistance
- **Aging:** Muscle mass decreases, adiposity increases, mitochondrial
  function declines
- **Genetics:** Variants in IRS1, PPARG, TCF7L2 genes impair insulin signaling

## Key Risk Factors

### 1. Obesity (BMI ≥ 30 kg/m²)
The most significant modifiable risk factor for Type 2 diabetes.
- BMI 30–35: ~5x increased risk
- BMI 35–40: ~7x increased risk  
- BMI ≥ 40 (Class III obesity): up to 15x increased risk

Visceral adiposity (fat around organs) is more dangerous than subcutaneous
fat. Even modest weight loss (5–10% of body weight) significantly improves
insulin sensitivity.

### 2. Elevated Blood Glucose (Hyperglycemia)
Pre-diabetes (fasting glucose 100–125 mg/dL or 2hr OGTT 140–199 mg/dL)
progresses to Type 2 diabetes in ~5–10% of people per year without
intervention. Early detection and lifestyle modification can prevent or
delay progression.

### 3. Hyperinsulinemia (Elevated Insulin)
High circulating insulin is both a marker of and a contributor to insulin
resistance. Chronically elevated insulin:
- Promotes fat storage (lipogenesis)
- Suppresses fat breakdown (lipolysis)
- Contributes to hypertension
- Increases cardiovascular risk

### 4. Hypertension (High Blood Pressure)
Strongly associated with insulin resistance through shared mechanisms:
- Insulin promotes sodium retention → blood pressure rises
- Sympathetic nervous system activation (common in obesity)
- Endothelial dysfunction
~73% of adults with diabetes have hypertension (blood pressure ≥ 130/80 mmHg)

### 5. Family History (Genetic Predisposition)
- First-degree relative with Type 2 diabetes: 2–3x increased risk
- Both parents with diabetes: ~50% lifetime risk
- Key genes: TCF7L2 (strongest genetic risk factor), PPARG, KCNJ11, HHEX
- Epigenetic factors also play a role

### 6. Gestational Diabetes History
Women who had gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM) have:
- 7–10x higher risk of developing Type 2 diabetes
- Risk is cumulative with multiple pregnancies
- Often indicates underlying insulin resistance revealed by pregnancy stress

### 7. Age
- Risk increases substantially after age 45
- Beta cell function declines ~1% per year after age 30
- Muscle mass decreases with age (sarcopenia), reducing glucose disposal

### 8. Ethnicity
Pima Native Americans (this dataset's population) have among the world's
highest rates of Type 2 diabetes (~50% prevalence), driven by:
- Genetic susceptibility developed over centuries of feast-famine cycles
- Rapid dietary changes in the 20th century (high-calorie Western diet)
- Physical activity reduction with modernization

## Prevention and Management

### Lifestyle Interventions (Primary Prevention):
- **Weight reduction:** 5–7% weight loss reduces diabetes progression by ~58%
  (Diabetes Prevention Program trial)
- **Physical activity:** 150 min/week moderate aerobic activity improves
  insulin sensitivity
- **Dietary modification:** Mediterranean diet, reduced refined carbohydrates
- **Sleep:** Poor sleep quality worsens insulin resistance

### Monitoring:
- HbA1c (glycated hemoglobin): reflects 3-month average glucose
  - Normal: < 5.7%
  - Pre-diabetes: 5.7–6.4%
  - Diabetes: ≥ 6.5%
- Fasting glucose monitoring
- Regular blood pressure checks
- Annual lipid panels

## Metabolic Syndrome
A cluster of conditions that together dramatically increase diabetes risk:
1. Abdominal obesity (waist > 102 cm men, > 88 cm women)
2. Elevated triglycerides (≥ 150 mg/dL)
3. Low HDL cholesterol
4. Hypertension (≥ 130/85 mmHg)
5. Elevated fasting glucose (≥ 100 mg/dL)

Having 3 or more of these criteria = metabolic syndrome diagnosis.
Metabolic syndrome increases diabetes risk by 5x.
