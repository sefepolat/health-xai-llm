# Clinical Guidelines for Diabetes Screening and Diagnosis

## Source References
- American Diabetes Association (ADA) Standards of Medical Care in Diabetes
- World Health Organization (WHO) Global Report on Diabetes
- International Diabetes Federation (IDF) Clinical Practice Recommendations

## Diagnostic Criteria for Diabetes (ADA 2024)

Diabetes is diagnosed by ANY of the following criteria:

### 1. Fasting Plasma Glucose (FPG)
- **Normal:** < 100 mg/dL
- **Pre-diabetes:** 100–125 mg/dL (Impaired Fasting Glucose - IFG)
- **Diabetes:** ≥ 126 mg/dL (confirmed on two separate occasions)

### 2. 2-Hour Plasma Glucose (2hr PG) During 75g OGTT
- **Normal:** < 140 mg/dL
- **Pre-diabetes:** 140–199 mg/dL (Impaired Glucose Tolerance - IGT)
- **Diabetes:** ≥ 200 mg/dL

### 3. HbA1c (Glycated Hemoglobin)
- **Normal:** < 5.7%
- **Pre-diabetes:** 5.7%–6.4%
- **Diabetes:** ≥ 6.5%

### 4. Random Plasma Glucose
- **Diabetes:** ≥ 200 mg/dL + classic hyperglycemia symptoms
  (polyuria, polydipsia, unexplained weight loss)

## Screening Recommendations (ADA 2024)

### Who Should Be Screened?
1. Adults aged ≥ 35 years → Screen at least every 3 years
2. Adults of any age with:
   - BMI ≥ 25 kg/m² (or ≥ 23 kg/m² in Asian Americans)
   - First-degree relative with diabetes
   - History of gestational diabetes
   - Hypertension (≥ 140/90 mmHg or on hypertension therapy)
   - HDL cholesterol < 35 mg/dL and/or triglycerides > 250 mg/dL
   - Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS)
   - Physical inactivity
   - Other clinical conditions associated with insulin resistance

### High-Risk Populations:
- Native American / Alaska Native populations: Begin screening at any age
- South Asian populations: Lower BMI thresholds apply (≥ 23 kg/m²)
- People with pre-diabetes: Annual screening recommended

## Pre-Diabetes Management

### Goal: Prevent or Delay Progression to Type 2 Diabetes

**Lifestyle Intervention (First-line):**
- Weight loss goal: 7% of body weight
- Physical activity: ≥ 150 minutes/week of moderate-intensity activity
  (e.g., brisk walking, cycling, swimming)
- Dietary approach: Reduce caloric intake, limit refined carbohydrates
  and saturated fats; increase fiber intake

**Evidence Base (Diabetes Prevention Program - DPP):**
- Intensive lifestyle intervention reduced diabetes incidence by 58%
  over 3 years in high-risk adults
- Effect was consistent across all racial/ethnic groups
- Long-term follow-up (10 years) confirmed sustained benefits

**Pharmacological Option:**
- Metformin may be considered for:
  - Those who cannot achieve lifestyle goals
  - BMI ≥ 35, age < 60, or women with prior GDM history
  - Does NOT replace lifestyle modification

## Treatment Goals for Diagnosed Diabetes

### Glycemic Targets (ADA 2024):
| Parameter | Target |
|---|---|
| HbA1c | < 7.0% (individualized) |
| Pre-meal glucose | 80–130 mg/dL |
| Post-meal glucose (2hr) | < 180 mg/dL |

### Blood Pressure Target:
- < 130/80 mmHg for most patients with diabetes
- < 140/90 mmHg acceptable in low cardiovascular risk patients

### BMI/Weight Goals:
- Weight loss of 5–15% improves glycemic control
- Bariatric surgery considered for BMI ≥ 40 or BMI 35–39.9 with
  inadequate glycemic control

## When to Refer to a Specialist

Patients should be referred to an endocrinologist or diabetes specialist when:
- Newly diagnosed with uncertain diabetes type
- HbA1c remains > 8% despite treatment
- Recurrent hypoglycemic episodes
- Suspected Type 1 diabetes or MODY (Maturity-Onset Diabetes of the Young)
- Pregnancy (gestational diabetes management)
- Complex comorbidities (kidney disease, heart failure)

## Diabetes Complications Screening

Patients with diagnosed diabetes require regular screening for:

| Complication | Screening Method | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| Diabetic nephropathy | Urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio + eGFR | Annual |
| Diabetic retinopathy | Dilated eye exam | Annual (or every 2yr if stable) |
| Diabetic neuropathy | Foot exam, monofilament test | Annual |
| Cardiovascular disease | Lipid panel, blood pressure | At least annual |
| Foot problems | Comprehensive foot exam | Annual |

## Lifestyle Recommendations for Patients

### Diet:
- **Mediterranean diet:** Strongest evidence for diabetes prevention and
  management; emphasizes vegetables, whole grains, legumes, fish, olive oil
- **Low-carbohydrate diet:** Effective for short-term glycemic improvement
- **Portion control:** Consistent meal timing improves glucose stability
- Avoid sugary beverages (soda, fruit juice, energy drinks)
- Limit processed and ultra-processed foods

### Physical Activity:
- **Aerobic exercise:** 150 min/week moderate intensity (or 75 min vigorous)
- **Resistance training:** 2–3 sessions/week improve insulin sensitivity
- **Reduce sedentary time:** Break sitting every 30 minutes with light movement
- **Benefits:** Each 10-minute bout of exercise improves glucose uptake

### Sleep:
- Both insufficient sleep (< 6 hours) and excessive sleep (> 9 hours)
  associated with higher diabetes risk
- Sleep apnea (very common in obesity) worsens insulin resistance; treatment
  with CPAP improves glucose control

### Stress Management:
- Chronic psychological stress raises cortisol → promotes insulin resistance
- Mindfulness-based stress reduction, counseling, and social support
  are beneficial adjuncts to lifestyle therapy
